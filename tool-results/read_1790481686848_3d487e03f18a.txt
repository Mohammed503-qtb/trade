     1→     1→# AI Market Reasoning Engine — Master Plan
     2→     2→
     3→     3→**Document status:** Authoritative architecture and build specification  
     4→     4→**Version:** 1.0  
     5→     5→**Date:** 2026-09-27  
     6→     6→**Owner:** System Architecture / Research / Execution  
     7→     7→**Primary objective:** Build a market-reasoning system that converts raw market information into auditable market state, evidence, scenarios, risk decisions, execution instructions, and post-trade learning without relying on brittle indicator stacking.
     8→     8→
     9→     9→---
    10→    10→
    11→    11→## 1. Project Definition
    12→    12→
    13→    13→AI Market Reasoning Engine is an adaptive, data-driven market analysis and decision system for short-horizon trading. It is designed to read price structure, liquidity, volume/footprint and order-flow proxies, session context, classical price action, harmonic structures, market-phase behavior, volatility, and macroeconomic context; fuse these observations into explicit competing scenarios; reject low-quality situations; convert only validated scenarios into risk-bounded trade intents; execute through a broker/exchange adapter only when execution constraints are acceptable; and record the complete causal chain so every decision can later be evaluated and learned from.
    14→    14→
    15→    15→It is **not** a collection of indicators, a black-box LLM that predicts the next candle, a proof that “smart money” can be seen directly from public chart data, a guarantee of profitability, or a system that treats the appearance of one named pattern as a trade signal. Terms such as Order Block, institutional zone, accumulation, manipulation, and distribution are treated as measurable **market-behavior hypotheses**, not as unverifiable claims about the identity or intention of market participants. The initial system is for research, analysis, paper trading, and tightly controlled live execution; any live use is gated by empirical validation after realistic costs.

    19→    19→## 2. Core Design Principles
    20→    20→
    21→    21→### 2.1 Causal chain
    22→    22→
    23→    23→Every production decision must be traceable through:
    24→    24→
    25→    25→`Raw Data → Data Quality → Market State → Context → Events → Evidence → Scenario → Risk Decision → Execution Intent → Fill/Outcome → Attribution → Learning`
    26→    26→
    27→    27→No module is allowed to create an isolated score that is not consumed by a downstream decision or stored for later evaluation.
    28→    28→
    29→    29→### 2.2 Evidence is contextual, not absolute
    30→    30→
    31→    31→A signal is never interpreted without location, regime, timing, volatility, and data quality. A bullish footprint inside a major bearish expansion can mean continuation, absorption, or trapped buyers depending on the surrounding auction.
    32→    32→
    33→    33→### 2.3 Independent evidence beats repeated evidence
    34→    34→
    35→    35→Five observations derived from the same price move do not equal five independent confirmations. Evidence Fusion therefore groups correlated observations and caps their combined contribution.
    36→    36→
    37→    37→### 2.4 Hard constraints before soft scores
    38→    38→
    39→    39→A hard risk or data-quality failure blocks a trade regardless of how attractive the analytical score is. Examples: stale feed, invalid instrument state, missing execution price, extreme spread, unavailable stop placement, or market halt.
    40→    40→
    41→    41→### 2.5 Scenario-first reasoning
    42→    42→
    43→    43→The engine generates competing explanations of the current market state rather than searching for a single predetermined direction.
    44→    44→
    45→    45→### 2.6 No magical confidence
    46→    46→
    47→    47→Before calibration, the engine uses evidence scores and grades, not pseudo-probabilities. A probability is exposed only after an explicit calibration procedure on out-of-sample data and is tracked by regime.
    48→    48→
    49→    49→### 2.7 Learning is controlled, not self-corrupting
    50→    50→
    51→    51→The live production policy never rewrites itself because of one trade or a short run of trades. Learning occurs through an immutable experience ledger, cohort statistics, offline re-estimation, walk-forward evaluation, promotion gates, and versioned deployment.
    52→    52→
    53→    53→### 2.8 Human-readable causality
    54→    54→
    55→    55→Every trade intent must have an explanation object containing: market context, liquidity map, structural state, supporting evidence, opposing evidence, trigger, invalidation, expected path, cost estimate, and reason for rejection when no trade is taken.
    56→    56→
    57→    57→---
    58→    58→
    59→    59→## 3. System Boundary and Responsibilities
    60→    60→
    61→    61→The platform is divided into six responsibility domains:
    62→    62→
    63→    63→1. **Understanding:** transform data into market facts and state.
    64→    64→2. **Decision:** compare scenarios and select whether a trade intent is justified.
    65→    65→3. **Risk:** determine whether the intent is allowed and size/shape it.
    66→    66→4. **Execution:** translate the approved intent into orders and verify actual fills.
    67→    67→5. **Learning:** measure what worked, what failed, why, and under which regime.
    68→    68→6. **Presentation:** render the state and reasoning to TradingView and the web dashboard.
    69→    69→
    70→    70→The boundaries are strict. The Understanding layer never submits orders. The Learning layer never directly mutates live trading parameters. Execution never invents market analysis to justify an order.

   156→   156→# 5. Technology Decisions
   157→   157→
   158→   158→## 5.1 Canonical language
   159→   159→
   160→   160→**Python 3.12+** is the canonical language for the external engine.
   161→   161→
   162→   162→Reasons:
   163→   163→
   164→   164→- Strong market-data and quantitative ecosystem.
   165→   165→- Efficient numerical work with NumPy/Polars.
   166→   166→- Mature APIs, async networking, testing, and ML tooling.
   167→   167→- Fast iteration for research while keeping production services in one language.
   168→   168→
   169→   169→**Pine Script v6** is used only for the TradingView component. It is not used as the central learning or execution platform.
   170→   170→
   171→   171→## 5.2 Storage
   172→   172→
   173→   173→**PostgreSQL + TimescaleDB** is the canonical relational/time-series store.
   174→   174→
   175→   175→Use it for:
   176→   176→
   177→   177→- normalized candles and market features;
   178→   178→- events, zones, scenarios, decisions;
   179→   179→- orders, fills, positions, and outcomes;
   180→   180→- model versions and parameters;
   181→   181→- calibration and drift statistics;
   182→   182→- audit logs.
   183→   183→
   184→   184→**S3-compatible object storage (MinIO in the first self-hosted deployment)** stores large immutable datasets:
   185→   185→
   186→   186→- raw trades;
   187→   187→- raw quote snapshots;
   188→   188→- compressed historical datasets;
   189→   189→- backtest exports;
   190→   190→- model artifacts;
   191→   191→- replay fixtures.
   192→   192→
   193→   193→Do not store every raw tick inside ordinary relational rows unless required by the data source; keep large raw streams in partitioned columnar files such as Parquet and store their metadata in PostgreSQL.
   194→   194→
   195→   195→## 5.3 Messaging
   196→   196→
   197→   197→**NATS JetStream** is the event bus.
   198→   198→
   199→   199→Reasons:
   200→   200→
   201→   201→- Low operational overhead compared with a full Kafka deployment.
   202→   202→- Durable streams and consumer groups are sufficient for the first production scale.
   203→   203→- Clear separation between ingestion, feature calculation, scenario decisions, execution, and learning.
   204→   204→
   205→   205→## 5.4 Hot state
   206→   206→
   207→   207→**Redis** is used for:
   208→   208→
   209→   209→- latest instrument state;
   210→   210→- short-lived locks;
   211→   211→- webhook idempotency keys;
   212→   212→- execution guards;
   213→   213→- rate limiting;
   214→   214→- cached session and macro state.
   215→   215→
   216→   216→Redis is not the system of record.
   217→   217→
   218→   218→## 5.5 API layer
   219→   219→
   220→   220→**FastAPI** exposes:
   221→   221→
   222→   222→- TradingView webhook endpoint;
   223→   223→- market-state API;
   224→   224→- scenario and decision API;
   225→   225→- operator dashboard API;
   226→   226→- execution status;
   227→   227→- research/replay controls.
   228→   228→
   229→   229→## 5.6 Research stack
   230→   230→
   231→   231→- Polars for high-performance tabular processing.
   232→   232→- NumPy for numerical arrays.
   233→   233→- SciPy for statistical procedures.
   234→   234→- scikit-learn for baseline models, calibration, metrics, and preprocessing.
   235→   235→- LightGBM for the first production-grade nonlinear ranking/classification learner after a deterministic baseline proves the feature set useful.
   236→   236→- PyTorch is deferred until a concrete problem is shown to benefit from sequence/deep models. It is not a default dependency.
   237→   237→
   238→   238→## 5.7 Frontend
   239→   239→
   240→   240→**React + TypeScript** web dashboard.
   241→   241→
   242→   242→The dashboard is an investigation and monitoring surface, not the source of truth.
   243→   243→
   244→   244→## 5.8 Deployment
   245→   245→
   246→   246→First production architecture uses Docker Compose on a persistent server with managed or separately backed-up PostgreSQL when available. Kubernetes is explicitly deferred until deployment scale or availability requirements prove it necessary.

   250→   250→# 6. TradingView Architecture
   251→   251→
   252→   252→## 6.1 Role of Pine v6
   253→   253→
   254→   254→The TradingView component has four responsibilities:
   255→   255→
   256→   256→1. Render canonical market concepts on the chart.
   257→   257→2. Run a lightweight, deterministic mirror of the most important event logic.
   258→   258→3. Create auditable alerts when configured conditions occur.
   259→   259→4. Act as a human-facing diagnostic surface.
   260→   260→
   261→   261→Pine is not responsible for:
   262→   262→
   263→   263→- long-term storage;
   264→   264→- model training;
   265→   265→- large-scale walk-forward jobs;
   266→   266→- portfolio-wide correlation analysis;
   267→   267→- broker reconciliation;
   268→   268→- live parameter mutation;
   269→   269→- large raw tick retention.
   270→   270→
   271→   271→## 6.2 Current relevant Pine constraints
   272→   272→
   273→   273→As of 2026-09-27, TradingView documents the following constraints relevant to this project:
   274→   274→
   275→   275→- Pine Script v6 is the current language.
   276→   276→- General `request.*()` calls are limited to 40 unique calls, or 64 on Ultimate plans; `request.footprint()` has its own one-unique-call restriction and requires Premium or Ultimate access.
   277→   277→- Script execution and loop execution are bounded; the documented total script execution limit is 20 seconds for basic accounts and 40 seconds for other accounts, with a 500 ms per-loop-per-bar limit.
   278→   278→- Compiled Pine code is limited to 100,256 compiled tokens; the compilation request has a 5 MB limit.
   279→   279→- Collections are limited to 100,000 elements.
   280→   280→- Lower-timeframe requests are capped by plan; current documentation lists up to 100K intrabars on non-professional plans, 125K on Expert, and 200K on Ultimate.
   281→   281→
   282→   282→These limits are reasons for keeping the external engine authoritative rather than trying to build the full reasoning and learning system in Pine.
   283→   283→
   284→   284→Official references:
   285→   285→
   286→   286→- https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/writing/limitations/
   287→   287→- https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/release-notes/
   288→   288→- https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/concepts/other-timeframes-and-data/
   289→   289→
   290→   290→## 6.3 Footprint capability
   291→   291→
   292→   292→TradingView now exposes footprint data to Pine through `request.footprint()`. The available data includes total buy volume, sell volume, delta, POC, VAH, VAL, and row-level imbalances. The project must use this capability where the instrument/feed supports it rather than reconstructing the same information from crude lower-timeframe guesses.
   293→   293→
   294→   294→A footprint request is a **bar-level observation**. It does not prove global market-wide order-book intent.
   295→   295→
   296→   296→Official reference:
   297→   297→
   298→   298→- https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/release-notes/
   299→   299→- https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/language/type-system/
   300→   300→
   301→   301→## 6.4 Webhook boundary
   302→   302→
   303→   303→TradingView alerts send HTTP POST requests to an external endpoint. JSON alert bodies are supported. Current TradingView documentation states webhook delivery uses ports 80/443, requests taking more than three seconds are cancelled, IPv6 is not currently supported for webhooks, and webhook delivery can occasionally fail; therefore the endpoint must respond quickly, persist the event, and process asynchronously.
   304→   304→
   305→   305→Webhook requirements:
   306→   306→
   307→   307→- HTTPS only.
   308→   308→- Authenticated endpoint.
   309→   309→- Replay/idempotency key.
   310→   310→- Schema version.
   311→   311→- Timestamp and alert event UUID.
   312→   312→- No secrets or credentials in the alert body.
   313→   313→- Fast acknowledgement; heavy work occurs asynchronously.
   314→   314→
   315→   315→Official reference:
   316→   316→
   317→   317→- https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000529348-how-to-configure-webhook-alerts/

   321→   321→# 7. Data Model and Market Data Contract
   322→   322→
   323→   323→The entire engine depends on a canonical event-time model.
   324→   324→
   325→   325→## 7.1 Time contract
   326→   326→
   327→   327→Every market record must contain:
   328→   328→
   329→   329→- `event_time_utc`: actual event timestamp when available.

   332→   332→- `venue`.
   333→   333→- `symbol`.
   334→   334→- `feed_id`.
   335→   335→- `sequence_id` when supplied by source.
   336→   336→- `source_latency_ms` where measurable.
   337→   337→
   338→   338→Never use local wall-clock time as market event time.
   339→   339→
   340→   340→## 7.2 Data hierarchy
   341→   341→
   342→   342→```text

   344→   344→        ↓

   346→   346→        ↓
   347→   347→Time-bucketed Bars

   357→   357→
   358→   358→## 7.3 Source hierarchy
   359→   359→
   360→   360→The engine distinguishes:
   361→   361→
   362→   362→1. Exchange/broker-native trade and quote data.
   363→   363→2. Specialized order-flow/market-depth data where licensed.
   364→   364→3. TradingView chart/footprint observations.
   365→   365→4. Economic releases from authoritative providers.

   373→   373→

   376→   376→- `PARTIAL`
   377→   377→- `DUPLICATED`
   378→   378→- `OUT_OF_ORDER`
   379→   379→- `STALE`
   380→   380→- `GAP_DETECTED`
   381→   381→- `UNAVAILABLE`
   382→   382→- `QUARANTINED`
   383→   383→
   384→   384→Only `HEALTHY` and explicitly approved `DELAYED` states can reach normal decision processing. Any data-quality degradation is attached to downstream evidence quality.

   439→   439→# 9. Context Engine
   440→   440→
   441→   441→The Context Engine answers: **What kind of market are we in?**
   442→   442→
   443→   443→It produces a structured state, not a trade direction.
   444→   444→
   445→   445→## 9.1 Timeframe hierarchy
   446→   446→
   447→   447→The engine uses three functional levels:
   448→   448→
   449→   449→- **Higher timeframe:** defines broad context and external structure.
   450→   450→- **Middle timeframe:** defines the setup and location.
   451→   451→- **Execution timeframe:** defines the trigger.
   452→   452→
   453→   453→Example reference stack:
   454→   454→
   455→   455→```text
   456→   456→HTF: 1H / 4H
   457→   457→MTF: 5m / 15m
   458→   458→LTF: 1m
   459→   459→```
   460→   460→
   461→   461→The exact selected periods are configuration values, but the role separation is invariant.
   462→   462→
   463→   463→## 9.2 HTF Bias
   464→   464→
   465→   465→HTF bias is a structured state determined from:
   466→   466→
   467→   467→- confirmed swing sequence;
   468→   468→- external structure direction;
   469→   469→- current dealing range;
   470→   470→- relationship to major liquidity;
   471→   471→- expansion/contraction state;
   472→   472→- higher-timeframe displacement;
   473→   473→- volatility regime.
   474→   474→
   475→   475→It is represented as:
   476→   476→
   477→   477→```text
   478→   478→BULLISH
   479→   479→BEARISH
   480→   480→NEUTRAL
   481→   481→TRANSITION
   482→   482→UNKNOWN
   483→   483→```
   484→   484→
   485→   485→HTF bias is contextual evidence. It does not trigger a trade.
   486→   486→
   487→   487→## 9.3 Market regime
   488→   488→
   489→   489→The engine classifies:
   490→   490→
   491→   491→- `TREND_EXPANSION`
   492→   492→- `TREND_PULLBACK`
   493→   493→- `RANGE_BALANCE`
   494→   494→- `RANGE_EXPANSION`
   495→   495→- `COMPRESSION`
   496→   496→- `VOLATILITY_SHOCK`
   497→   497→- `TRANSITION`
   498→   498→- `UNKNOWN`
   499→   499→
   500→   500→Regime features include normalized range, directional efficiency, volatility percentile, swing persistence, volume concentration, and displacement frequency.
   501→   501→
   502→   502→## 9.4 Session context
   503→   503→
   504→   504→Sessions are explicit objects with:
   505→   505→
   506→   506→- open time;
   507→   507→- close time;
   508→   508→- opening range;
   509→   509→- initial balance where meaningful;
   510→   510→- prior session high/low;
   511→   511→- current session high/low;
   512→   512→- session volume profile where available;
   513→   513→- overlap flags;
   514→   514→- session transition risk.
   515→   515→
   516→   516→Session is contextual metadata. It is not a direction signal by itself.
   517→   517→
   518→   518→## 9.5 Opening structure
   519→   519→
   520→   520→The system tracks:
   521→   521→
   522→   522→- gap relative to prior session where meaningful;
   523→   523→- opening range;
   524→   524→- opening drive;
   525→   525→- opening reversal;
   526→   526→- early liquidity formation;
   527→   527→- opening imbalance;
   528→   528→- prior close relationship.
   529→   529→
   530→   530→No fixed assumption is made that a gap must fill or that an opening drive must continue.
   531→   531→
   532→   532→---
   533→   533→
   534→   534→# 10. Liquidity Map Engine
   535→   535→
   536→   536→The Liquidity Map represents **where price has structural reasons to encounter opposing interest or stop-driven activity**.
   537→   537→
   538→   538→It is an inferred map, not direct knowledge of every participant's orders.
   539→   539→
   540→   540→## 10.1 Liquidity sources
   541→   541→
   542→   542→### Sell-side candidates
   543→   543→
   544→   544→- prior swing lows;
   545→   545→- equal lows;
   546→   546→- session lows;
   547→   547→- previous day/week lows;
   548→   548→- range boundaries;
   549→   549→- obvious repeated lows;
   550→   550→- untested external liquidity.
   551→   551→
   552→   552→### Buy-side candidates
   553→   553→
   554→   554→- prior swing highs;
   555→   555→- equal highs;
   556→   556→- session highs;
   557→   557→- previous day/week highs;
   558→   558→- range boundaries;
   559→   559→- obvious repeated highs;
   560→   560→- untested external liquidity.
   561→   561→
   562→   562→## 10.2 Liquidity zone object
   563→   563→
   564→   564→```text
   565→   565→zone_id
   566→   566→side
   567→   567→price_low
   568→   568→price_high
   569→   569→origin_time
   570→   570→age
   571→   571→source_type
   572→   572→test_count
   573→   573→last_test_time
   574→   574→sweep_status
   575→   575→reaction_score
   576→   576→unmitigated_score
   577→   577→importance_score
   578→   578→```
   579→   579→
   580→   580→## 10.3 Zone strength
   581→   581→
   582→   582→Zone strength is calculated from:
   583→   583→
   584→   584→- structural importance;
   585→   585→- number and spacing of equal/repeated levels;
   586→   586→- freshness;
   587→   587→- timeframe significance;
   588→   588→- distance to current price;
   589→   589→- previous reaction quality;
   590→   590→- whether the zone has already been consumed.
   591→   591→
   592→   592→The score is not treated as probability.
   593→   593→
   594→   594→## 10.4 Sweep detection
   595→   595→
   596→   596→A sweep is a sequence, not a single wick.
   597→   597→
   598→   598→Minimum structure:
   599→   599→
   600→   600→1. A previously identified liquidity zone exists.
   601→   601→2. Price trades through the zone by a regime-normalized excursion.
   602→   602→3. The excursion reaches a threshold relative to local volatility and zone width.
   603→   603→4. Price either reclaims the zone or shows a continuation response that distinguishes a sweep from a genuine break.
   604→   604→5. The event is timestamped and linked to the consumed liquidity zone.
   605→   605→
   606→   606→A sweep may be classified:
   607→   607→
   608→   608→- `FAILED_SWEEP`
   609→   609→- `PARTIAL_SWEEP`
   610→   610→- `CONFIRMED_SWEEP`
   611→   611→- `BREAK_AND_ACCEPT`
   612→   612→- `UNKNOWN`
   613→   613→
   614→   614→## 10.5 Target mapping
   615→   615→
   616→   616→After an active setup is created, the engine computes the next relevant liquidity targets on both sides. Scenario generation uses these targets to estimate the plausible path and invalidation distance.
   617→   617→
   618→   618→---
   619→   619→
   620→   620→# 11. Structure and SMC Engine
   621→   621→
   622→   622→SMC concepts are implemented only when they become measurable state transitions.
   623→   623→
   624→   624→## 11.1 Swing detection
   625→   625→
   626→   626→A swing is confirmed only after a defined structural lookback/confirmation rule is satisfied. The rule must not rely on future bars in live mode beyond the required confirmation delay.
   627→   627→
   628→   628→Each swing contains:
   629→   629→
   630→   630→```text
   631→   631→swing_id
   632→   632→price
   633→   633→timeframe
   634→   634→direction
   635→   635→strength
   636→   636→confirmation_time
   637→   637→external_or_internal
   638→   638→```
   639→   639→
   640→   640→## 11.2 BOS
   641→   641→
   642→   642→**Break of Structure (BOS)** is a confirmed breach of a structurally relevant prior swing in the current directional framework.
   643→   643→
   644→   644→Programmable condition:
   645→   645→
   646→   646→- identify a confirmed swing;
   647→   647→- require a close or accepted trade beyond the swing by a configurable minimum displacement threshold;
   648→   648→- verify the break is not merely an isolated wick unless a strategy explicitly treats wick breaks as valid;
   649→   649→- classify as internal or external.
   650→   650→
   651→   651→Measure:
   652→   652→
   653→   653→- breach distance normalized by ATR/local range;
   654→   654→- closing acceptance;
   655→   655→- volume/flow support;
   656→   656→- follow-through.
   657→   657→
   658→   658→## 11.3 CHoCH
   659→   659→
   660→   660→**Change of Character (CHoCH)** is a structure transition in which the current swing sequence first violates the prior directional continuation pattern.
   661→   661→
   662→   662→It is weaker than a confirmed regime reversal and should not be treated as one automatically.
   663→   663→
   664→   664→## 11.4 Displacement
   665→   665→
   666→   666→Displacement measures unusually directional price travel relative to recent volatility.
   667→   667→
   668→   668→Core features:
   669→   669→
   670→   670→```text
   671→   671→range_zscore
   672→   672→body_fraction
   673→   673→close_location
   674→   674→ATR_multiple
   675→   675→velocity
   676→   676→follow_through
   677→   677→```
   678→   678→
   679→   679→A displacement event requires a directional range/impulse above a rolling percentile and meaningful closing efficiency.
   680→   680→
   681→   681→## 11.5 Fair Value Gap (FVG)
   682→   682→
   683→   683→For a three-bar sequence:
   684→   684→
   685→   685→- bullish FVG candidate: current low > high of bar two-bars-back;
   686→   686→- bearish FVG candidate: current high < low of bar two-bars-back.
   687→   687→
   688→   688→The gap is stored as a price interval with:
   689→   689→
   690→   690→- origin time;
   691→   691→- direction;
   692→   692→- size normalized by local ATR;
   693→   693→- fill percentage;
   694→   694→- first mitigation time;
   695→   695→- invalidation/consumption state.
   696→   696→
   697→   697→The engine does not assume every FVG must fill.
   698→   698→
   699→   699→## 11.6 Order Block
   700→   700→
   701→   701→An Order Block is defined operationally as a bounded pre-displacement consolidation/candle region that precedes a structurally meaningful displacement and remains relevant after the move.
   702→   702→
   703→   703→The detection pipeline is:
   704→   704→
   705→   705→1. Identify a confirmed displacement.
   706→   706→2. Trace backward to the final opposing candle or compact opposing cluster before the impulse.
   707→   707→3. Verify the impulse generated meaningful structure change or liquidity interaction.
   708→   708→4. Define the source zone.
   709→   709→5. Track retests and reactions.
   710→   710→
   711→   711→Quality depends on:
   712→   712→
   713→   713→- displacement magnitude;
   714→   714→- structural consequence;
   715→   715→- freshness;
   716→   716→- retest response;
   717→   717→- volume/flow behavior.
   718→   718→
   719→   719→An “Order Block” is not interpreted as proof of institutional order placement.
   720→   720→
   721→   721→## 11.7 Premium / Discount
   722→   722→
   723→   723→For a defined dealing range:
   724→   724→
   725→   725→```text
   726→   726→equilibrium = (range_high + range_low) / 2
   727→   727→premium = price > equilibrium
   728→   728→discount = price < equilibrium
   729→   729→```
   730→   730→
   731→   731→The dealing range must be explicitly named. The engine refuses ambiguous “premium/discount” calculations that change range definition silently.
   732→   732→
   733→   733→## 11.8 Inducement
   734→   734→
   735→   735→Inducement is not implemented as a mystical label. It is represented as a **candidate liquidity-building pattern** where price creates a local obvious level before a larger structural event. It only contributes evidence when the subsequent liquidity interaction validates the hypothesis.
   736→   736→
   737→   737→---
   738→   738→
   739→   739→# 12. Order Flow and Footprint Engine
   740→   740→
   741→   741→Order Flow answers: **What did trading activity look like at price, and did price respond accordingly?**
   742→   742→
   743→   743→## 12.1 Core metrics
   744→   744→
   745→   745→- total volume;
   746→   746→- buy volume;
   747→   747→- sell volume;
   748→   748→- delta;
   749→   749→- buy/sell share;
   750→   750→- POC;
   751→   751→- VAH/VAL;
   752→   752→- row-level delta;
   753→   753→- buy imbalance;
   754→   754→- sell imbalance;
   755→   755→- volume concentration;
   756→   756→- delta trend.
   757→   757→
   758→   758→## 12.2 Delta
   759→   759→
   760→   760→```text
   761→   761→delta = buy_volume - sell_volume
   762→   762→```
   763→   763→
   764→   764→The important observation is not the sign alone. The engine compares delta to price result.
   765→   765→
   766→   766→### Effort vs Result
   767→   767→
   768→   768→Examples:
   769→   769→
   770→   770→- large positive delta + strong price rise → aggressive buying accepted;
   771→   771→- large positive delta + weak price response → possible absorption/trapped buying;
   772→   772→- large negative delta + weak price decline → possible sell absorption;
   773→   773→- large volume + narrow range → possible two-sided auction or absorption.
   774→   774→
   775→   775→These are hypotheses that must be confirmed by subsequent response.
   776→   776→
   777→   777→## 12.3 Absorption
   778→   778→
   779→   779→Absorption is detected as a mismatch between aggressive traded volume and realized price movement at a meaningful location.
   780→   780→
   781→   781→A candidate requires:
   782→   782→
   783→   783→1. elevated directional delta or volume at a zone;
   784→   784→2. limited price extension relative to the aggression;
   785→   785→3. repeated response or failure to continue;
   786→   786→4. optional subsequent displacement in the opposite direction.
   787→   787→
   788→   788→Absorption quality increases when the event occurs at mapped liquidity or structural boundaries.
   789→   789→
   790→   790→## 12.4 Exhaustion
   791→   791→
   792→   792→Exhaustion is a decline in directional effectiveness:
   793→   793→
   794→   794→- declining extension per unit of directional volume;
   795→   795→- reducing follow-through;
   796→   796→- repeated failed highs/lows;
   797→   797→- deteriorating delta efficiency.
   798→   798→
   799→   799→Exhaustion alone is not a reversal signal.
